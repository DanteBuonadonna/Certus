# Failed the Series 7? Retake Rules, Waiting Periods, and What to Do Next

If you failed the Series 7, you can retake it 30 days after your first or second failed attempt. If you fail three or more times within a two-year period, the wait jumps to 180 days. Each retake is a brand-new exam with the full $395 fee, and your firm has to enroll you again.

That's the short answer. There's also a change coming: FINRA has already amended the rule to cut those waits roughly in half, but it hasn't switched the new timelines on yet. Here's how the rules work right now, what's changing, and how to use the waiting period so the next attempt is your last one.

## The current Series 7 retake rules

FINRA's waiting periods come from Rule 1210, and they apply to the specific exam you failed, not to every exam. Failing the Series 7 doesn't stop you from taking a different exam during the wait.

| Failed attempt | Wait before you can retake |
|---|---|
| 1st | 30 calendar days |
| 2nd | 30 calendar days |
| 3rd or later, within a two-year period | 180 calendar days |

A few details people miss:

- **There's no lifetime cap on attempts.** The penalty for repeated failures is time, not a ban. FINRA itself doesn't cap your attempts, but your firm might, so ask your manager what its policy is.
- **The 180-day rule is about failures inside a two-year window.** Three failures spread over several years don't trigger it the same way three failures in eight months do.
- **You pay again every time.** The Series 7 fee is $395 per attempt. There's no retake discount.
- **Your SIE isn't affected.** The SIE and Series 7 are corequisites. A passed SIE stays valid for four years whatever happens with the Series 7.

## What's changing: FINRA's shorter waiting periods

On June 29, 2026, FINRA filed an amendment to Rule 1210 with the SEC that shortens the waiting periods for all FINRA qualification exams, including the Series 7 and the SIE:

| Failed attempt | Current wait | New wait (once implemented) |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 30 days | 15 days |
| 2nd | 30 days | 15 days |
| 3rd or later, within two years | 180 days | 60 days |

Here's the catch: the rule took effect when it was filed, but **the shorter waits aren't live for candidates yet.** FINRA has said it will announce an implementation date in a future Regulatory Notice, and until then the 30/30/180 schedule still applies. At the time of writing, that date hasn't been announced. If you're planning a retake, check FINRA's site for the notice before you count on the shorter wait.

The change covers FINRA exams only. The Series 63, 65, and 66 are NASAA exams, so they still use the older waiting periods for now.

**A practical warning:** even when the 15-day wait goes live, that doesn't mean you should retake in 15 days. Being *allowed* to retake sooner is only useful if you've actually fixed what went wrong. Most people who fail need more than two weeks to close a real gap.

## Step one: read your score report before you do anything else

When you finish a FINRA exam, you get a results report at the test center. If you didn't pass, it shows your overall score and how you did in each major section of the content outline. That breakdown is the most useful study tool you'll get, so don't throw it away.

The Series 7 is organized around four job functions, and they aren't close to equal in weight:

| Function | What it covers | Share of exam | Scored questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Seeking business for the broker-dealer | 7% | 9 |
| F2 | Opening accounts and evaluating customer profiles | 9% | 11 |
| F3 | Investment information, suitable recommendations, records | 73% | 91 |
| F4 | Taking orders, processing and confirming transactions | 11% | 14 |

The passing score is 72, which works out to about 90 of the 125 scored questions. (The exam also includes a handful of unscored pretest questions, and you can't tell which ones they are.)

Look at those numbers again. **Function 3 is 91 questions.** If your report shows weakness there, that's almost certainly why you failed, and it's where nearly all of your retake hours should go. F3 is where options strategies, municipal bonds, investment company products, suitability, and the math live. Someone who scored 66 with a weak F3 has a very different problem than someone who scored 66 with weak F1 and F2.

## How to diagnose what actually went wrong

A score report tells you *where* you lost points. You still have to figure out *why*. Most failed attempts fall into one of these patterns:

**You knew it but ran out of time or focus.** The Series 7 is 225 minutes long, and stamina matters. If your practice scores were solid but your real score wasn't, you probably need more full-length timed practice, not more content review.

**You recognized the material but couldn't apply it.** This is the most common pattern on F3. Reading about a bear put spread is different from working out breakeven and max loss under time pressure. The fix is reps: hundreds of applied questions, especially on options and suitability.

**You had real content gaps.** Some topics, like options, municipal securities, and margin math, need focused, from-scratch work. If one of these is a black box, rereading your notes won't fix it. Rebuild it from the ground up.

**You took it before you were ready.** Firms often push for a fast turnaround. If you went in averaging below the passing line on practice exams, the result wasn't bad luck. The plan was too short.

## A realistic retake plan

Under the current rules you have a 30-day floor, but that's a minimum, not a target. A reasonable structure:

1. **Days 1–3: Debrief.** Write down everything you remember about the questions that tripped you up while it's fresh. Match those notes against your score report.
2. **Weeks 1–3: Fix the biggest weakness.** Put most of your time into your weakest high-weight area, which usually means F3. Do targeted question sets, not chapter rereads.
3. **Weeks 3–5: Full-length practice.** Take timed, full-length practice exams. Don't schedule the retake until you're consistently scoring comfortably above 72, not just scraping it.
4. **Final week: Light review.** Flashcards and missed-question review. Don't cram new material.
