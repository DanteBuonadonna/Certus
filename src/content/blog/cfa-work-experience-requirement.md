# CFA Work Experience Requirement: What Counts (2026)

To use the CFA designation, you need **at least 4,000 hours of qualifying work experience, completed over a minimum of 36 months**, on top of passing all three exam levels. The work can happen before, during, or after the CFA Program, and it qualifies if it's directly involved in investment decision-making or produces work that informs that process.

That one-sentence definition leaves a lot of room for interpretation, and it's where many candidates get stuck after Level III. Below is what CFA Institute actually looks for, how your hours get counted, and how to describe your job so the application goes through.

## The three rules, in plain English

CFA Institute reviews every membership application against three requirements:

| Requirement | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Length of time** | At least 4,000 hours, completed over a minimum of 36 months |
| **Relevance** | Hours must be directly related to the investment decision-making process, or produce a work product that informs or adds value to it |
| **Work arrangement** | Full-time, part-time, or remote work all count, whether it happened before, during, or after the CFA Program |

The 36-month floor is the part people miss. Even if you work 60-hour weeks in equity research, you can't bank 4,000 hours in two years and apply. The hours *and* the calendar both have to be satisfied.

The flip side is good news: the clock doesn't start when you pass Level I. If you've already spent three years as a credit analyst before you enroll, that experience can count. Many candidates finish their hours long before they finish the exams.

## What kind of work qualifies

CFA Institute publishes a self-assessment with ten guiding questions. If you can answer "yes" to any one of them for the majority of your duties in a role, your experience is likely to qualify. Paraphrased, they ask whether you:

1. Build or rebalance client portfolios, prepare financial models, or do independent analysis that leads to an investment decision
2. Make investment decisions using financial, economic, or statistical data on securities held in a portfolio
3. Supervise people who do the work in points 1 or 2
4. Work in corporate finance: capital structure, funding decisions, long-range financial planning, valuing securities for offerings or M&A, or capital investment decisions
5. Provide investment or financial advice or planning to corporations, institutions, or individuals (not yourself or your family)
6. Do asset allocation analysis or build and explain investment policy statements and strategies
7. Oversee investment compliance, perform due diligence, or make sure investment firms follow laws and professional standards
8. Research or build tools, models, or strategies that help portfolio managers and traders with decisions and risk management
9. Teach finance full-time as a professor or instructor, including research on investment practice
10. Design and implement software that directly drives investment decisions, like platforms used to build financial models or manage and analyze portfolios

That list is broader than most people expect. Equity research, credit analysis, portfolio management, and investment banking are the obvious fits. But financial advisors, investment compliance officers, due-diligence analysts, corporate finance teams, quant developers, and finance professors can all qualify too.

What tends *not* to qualify: roles that touch finance without shaping an investment decision. Pure operations, client service with no analytical component, general accounting, or sales work that doesn't involve advice usually fall short on their own.

If none of the ten questions fit your current job, CFA Institute's answer is that your experience doesn't qualify yet. You can still join a local society as an Affiliate member while you build qualifying hours.

## How your hours are actually calculated

You don't submit a timesheet. CFA Institute calculates your hours from **the percentage of each working week you spend on investment decision-making activities**, multiplied across the number of months you've held each position.

That has two practical consequences:

- **A hybrid role can still count, partially.** If you're a corporate FP&A analyst and roughly half your week goes to capital investment analysis, that half is what gets credited. Be honest about the split; it's the number your application is built on.
- **Each position is assessed separately.** CFA Institute asks you to evaluate every role you've held, even different roles at the same company. A promotion from operations into a research seat starts a new, qualifying entry.

On internships: CFA Institute's guidance is that paid internships can count when the work itself qualifies. If you're relying on internship hours, check the current rules in CFA Institute's help center before you assume they'll be credited.

## How to write a work description that gets approved

This is where applications stall. CFA Institute's own examples show the difference between a description that passes and one that gets sent back.

**Qualifying:** "I prepare financial models based on reviewed information, analyze fundamentals, and value acquisition targets to provide recommendations to the investment committee."

It works because it names the independent analysis (models, fundamentals, valuation) and the work product that influences a decision (recommendations to the committee).

**Non-qualifying:** "My work focuses on successfully managing key relationships with end-clients relating to a new Know Your Client (KYC) initiative."

It fails because it doesn't show independent analysis or a work product that affects investment decisions. The fix CFA Institute suggests is describing the advice given, the assessment of client financials, and the stress testing behind it.

Their actuarial example makes the same point: "I conducted an annual actuarial valuation" was not enough on its own. Adding that the projections and duration analysis "had a direct bearing on the investments chosen to back the reserves" made the link to investment decisions explicit.

The pattern: **say what analysis you do, then say which investment decision it feeds.** If your title sounds non-investment, the second half carries the whole application.

## References, review time, and cost

Alongside your work history, you'll need professional references:

- **Two references**, if one of them is an active professional or charterholder member of the local society you're applying to, or
- **Three references**, if none of them are

You apply through a local CFA Society, and the review happens in two stages: CFA Institute first (up to 10 business days), then the society (which can take up to 30 calendar days). CFA Institute says the full review can take up to six weeks once all your references are in, so chasing slow references is often the real bottleneck.

Once approved, you activate membership by signing a Professional Conduct Statement and Membership Agreement and paying dues. Full-year CFA Institute dues are **$299**, prorated by the month you join, and the membership year runs July 1 to June 30. Local society dues are separate.

## Common questions

**Do I need the work experience before I can take the exams?**
No. The work requirement applies to using the charter, not to enrolling. You can pass all three levels first and finish your hours afterward. (Student candidates have their own enrollment rules, covered in [can you take the CFA in college](/blog/can-you-take-the-cfa-in-college).)

**Does it have to be continuous?**
No. Hours can come from multiple positions and employers, as long as the total reaches 4,000 over at least 36 months.

**Can I call myself a CFA after passing Level III?**
Not yet. Passing the exams, completing the Practical Skills Modules, meeting the work requirement, and being admitted as a member are all required. We walk through the full checklist in [what is a CFA charterholder](/blog/what-is-a-cfa-charterholder).
