# How Many Times Can You Retake the CFA Exam?

You get **six total attempts per CFA exam level**. Within that, CFA Institute's eligibility policy allows a maximum of **two attempts per calendar year** (when a level is offered often enough), and those attempts can't fall in consecutive testing windows or in windows less than six months apart. Fail a level six times and you're no longer eligible to register for it.

Those are the rules as CFA Institute words them in its CFA Exam Eligibility Policy. Most candidates never come close to the cap, but the spacing rules shape your retake timeline more than the six-attempt limit does. Here's how it all works.

## The rules, in plain English

From CFA Institute's eligibility policy:

- **Six attempts per level.** Level I, Level II, and Level III each have their own count of six. Failing Level I twice doesn't reduce your attempts at Level II.
- **No more than two per calendar year.** This applies "when applicable," meaning for levels offered more than once a year.
- **Not in consecutive windows, and not less than six months apart.** You can't fail in one window and immediately try again in the next one.
- **Partial attempts count.** If you start the exam but only complete one session, that still counts toward your six.
- **The cap follows you.** After six unsuccessful attempts at a level, you can't register for it again, whether under your existing account or a new one.

CFA Institute's policy doesn't state a separate lifetime limit beyond the six-per-level rule, and it doesn't address how many years you have to finish the program in the pages we reviewed. If you're planning around a long gap between levels, check the current policy pages directly before you commit.

## What the spacing rule means for each level

The windows differ by level. Level I is offered in February, May, August, and November. Level II is offered in May, August, and November. Level III is offered in February and August. (Exact dates change, so check our [CFA exam registration deadlines](/blog/cfa-exam-registration-deadlines) guide for the current calendar.)

Putting the rules together with those windows:

**Level I** has the most flexibility. If you fail in August, November is off-limits (consecutive window), but February is open, and so is May. If you fail in February, you can look at August or November rather than May. Practically, your fastest retake is about six months out.

**Level II** has fewer windows, so the wait can stretch. A May attempt followed by a November attempt satisfies the six-month rule. After a failed August attempt, though, the next Level II window you can use is May of the following year.

**Level III** has just two windows a year, February and August, and they're consecutive windows of each other. Our reading is that this makes roughly one attempt per year the realistic pace for Level III, though the registration system is the final word on whether a given window is open to you. Confirm there before you build a plan around a second sitting.

Either way, six attempts at the pace of one a year is a very long runway, which is the real point: the cap exists, but the calendar slows you down well before it does.

## How often do people actually need a retake?

Often enough that you should plan for it as a possibility, not a disaster. CFA Institute's published results show that fewer than half of candidates pass in many windows. Level I was 43% in August 2026. Level II was 43% in May 2026, with a 10-year average of about 45%. Level III was 50% in February 2026, with a 10-year average of 51%.

Those figures include people who already tried before. Many windows show first-time candidates passing at higher rates than the overall number, which means retakers and deferred candidates pull the average down. Our [Level II pass rate](/blog/cfa-level-2-pass-rate) and [Level III pass rate](/blog/cfa-level-3-pass-rate) breakdowns show the split.

## What a retake really costs

The attempt cap is rarely the expensive part. Every attempt means paying the registration fee again, and the fee depends on when you register: early registration is cheaper than the standard deadline. For Level I or II, early registration is $1,140 and the final-deadline fee is $350 higher; Level III is $1,240 early. Those fees and deadlines are in our [registration deadlines guide](/blog/cfa-exam-registration-deadlines), and you should confirm them on CFA Institute's dates and fees page before you register.

Also note that **starting with the February 2027 exams, CFA Institute is eliminating paid deferrals.** If you register and don't take the exam, you forfeit the fee. A no-show doesn't give you a free extra attempt, but it also isn't automatically counted as one of your six, so check the policy if you're ever in that spot. Our [deferral guide](/blog/cfa-exam-deferral-policy) covers the details.

## How to avoid using attempts you don't need

Because each attempt costs over a thousand dollars and at least six months, the best strategy is making the first one count.

1. **Take a full timed mock early.** It shows your real baseline while you still have time to fix it. See our [mock exam strategy guide](/blog/cfa-level-1-mock-exam-strategy).
2. **Practice more than you read.** The exam tests application under time pressure, not recognition.
3. **Register for a window you'll actually take.** With paid deferrals ending, a realistic date beats an optimistic one.
4. **If you do fail, read your score report before choosing a window.** Our guide on [what to do after failing Level 1](/blog/failed-cfa-level-1-what-to-do-next) walks through it.
