# How to Register for the SIE Exam (Without a Firm)

To register for the SIE exam, create an individual account in FINRA's Test Enrollment Services System (TESS), enroll for the SIE and pay the $100 fee, then schedule your appointment through Prometric — either at a test center or online. You don't need a job, a sponsoring firm, or U.S. citizenship; you just need to be 18 or older.

That's the short version. The details below are the ones that trip people up: the 120-day clock, the identity information that has to match later, and what happens to your money if you miss your window.

## Who can register for the SIE

The Securities Industry Essentials exam is FINRA's one exam that's open to the public. Per FINRA's own FAQ, the eligibility list is short:

- **You must be at least 18.**
- **You don't need to be associated with a broker-dealer.** No sponsor, no Form U4.
- **You don't need to be a U.S. citizen.**
- **You can't have an open SIE enrollment already,** and you can't have a valid SIE pass on record (a pass is good for four years).
- **You can't currently be registered in Web CRD.** If you already work at a FINRA member firm in a registered role, your firm enrolls you instead of you doing it yourself.

That last point matters for anyone who has just been hired. If your firm is already filing your paperwork, ask your registration department before you pay anything — firms can enroll candidates directly or hand out prepaid SIE vouchers.

## SIE exam at a glance

| Detail | What FINRA says |
|---|---|
| Fee | $100 |
| Questions | 75 scored (plus 10 unscored pretest items) |
| Time | 1 hour 45 minutes |
| Passing score | 70 |
| Enrollment window | 120 calendar days to schedule and take it |
| Result validity | 4 years from the date you pass |
| Delivery | Prometric test center or online |

## Step-by-step: how to register for the SIE

### 1. Create your FINRA account

Start at FINRA's SIE page and click **Get Started** under the "Individuals" enrollment option. You'll create an account (by email or with a Google login), set security questions, and agree to FINRA's privacy statement.

To build your profile you'll need:

- Your Social Security number (or, if you don't have one, your mother's maiden name)
- Your date of birth
- A primary and a secondary phone number
- Your current residential address

FINRA notes that enrollment works best on a **Windows laptop or desktop** — users have reported problems enrolling from Macs, phones, and tablets. If the site misbehaves, switch machines before you call support.

**Get your identity details exactly right.** This is the step that bites people years later. When a broker-dealer eventually hires you and files your Form U4, the information on it has to match what you entered for the SIE. If it doesn't, FINRA says your result may not import into your CRD record — and you could be asked to retest. If you have an SSN, use it. If you typo it, call the FINRA Support Center rather than leaving it.

### 2. Enroll and pay the $100 fee

Once your profile is set, select the SIE and pay by credit card, debit card, or ACH. If a school, employer, or training program gave you a **voucher code**, enter it and click Apply before continuing — otherwise the system routes you to the payment page.

Two money rules to know before you click "pay":

- **The fee is non-refundable.** FINRA doesn't accept refund requests once payment processes, and you can't transfer it to another exam or another person.
- **ACH is slower.** It can take up to 48 hours to process, versus near-immediate for a card.

Most requests are processed within a couple of business days. When yours is approved, you'll get a Notice of Enrollment email with your unique ID number — you need that ID, plus your first and last name, to book with Prometric.

Military veterans: FINRA notes that GI Bill–eligible veterans may be able to get the SIE fee reimbursed through the VA.

### 3. Schedule your appointment within 120 days

Your enrollment is valid for **120 calendar days** from approval. Inside that window you need to both schedule *and* take the exam. Once you're approved, a **Schedule Exam** button appears on your "My Exams" page in TESS, which hands you off to Prometric.

You'll pick between:

- **A Prometric test center** — the traditional option, and the one to choose if your home internet or workspace is unreliable.
- **Online proctored testing** — from home, with a webcam and a clean, private room.

If you let the 120 days run out, the enrollment expires and you start over with a new request and a new $100 payment. The same is true for no-shows. Prometric also treats cancellations close to your appointment differently from ones made well in advance, so check the cancellation terms on your confirmation before you lock in a date.

**Practical tip:** don't enroll the day you start studying. Most candidates need a few weeks to a couple of months of prep, so enroll when you're roughly 30–60 days from being ready. That leaves margin for a reschedule without burning your window.

### 4. Take the exam and get your result

You'll see a preliminary pass/fail result as soon as you finish. Your official result and test date show up under "My Exams" in TESS within 72 hours. If you fail, FINRA gives you performance feedback by topic so you know where to focus.

## If you fail: retake rules

FINRA's SIE FAQ lists the current waiting periods:

- **After your 1st and 2nd failed attempts:** wait 30 days
- **After your 3rd failed attempt (and every attempt after that):** wait 180 days

To retake, you submit a brand-new enrollment and pay the fee again. FINRA filed a rule change in mid-2026 that would shorten these waits, but as of this writing it hasn't announced an implementation date — so plan around 30 and 180 days. ## What the SIE covers (so you know what you're paying for)

FINRA's content outline splits the 75 scored questions into four sections:

| Section | Share | Questions |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge of Capital Markets | 16% | 12 |
| Understanding Products and Their Risks | 44% | 33 |
| Understanding Trading, Customer Accounts and Prohibited Activities | 31% | 23 |
| Overview of Regulatory Framework | 9% | 7 |

Products and their risks is nearly half the exam, so that's where your study time should go first. For a fuller look at difficulty and prep time, see [how hard is the SIE exam](/blog/how-hard-is-the-sie-exam).

## What passing does (and doesn't) get you

Passing the SIE does **not** license you to sell securities, and it won't put you on BrokerCheck. It's a corequisite: to actually register as a representative, you also need to pass a qualification exam like the Series 7 — and *that* one requires a sponsoring firm. If you're weighing the two, our [SIE vs Series 7 breakdown](/blog/sie-vs-series-7-difference) walks through the differences.

What the SIE *does* give you is a four-year credential you can earn before anyone hires you. For college students and career changers, that's a real line on a résumé and one fewer exam your future employer has to wait on.

## The bottom line

Registering for the SIE is mostly paperwork: make a FINRA account, pay $100, and book with Prometric inside 120 days. The parts worth slowing down for are entering your identity details exactly as they'll appear on a future U4, and not opening your window until you're close to ready.
