# Which CFA Level 1 Exam Window Should You Take?

Take the window that gives you at least 300 hours of consistent study time before exam day, not the one with the best pass rate. Level I is offered four times a year (usually February, May, August, and November), and the pass-rate differences between windows are small enough that your preparation time matters far more than the calendar.

Here's the data, the real tradeoffs between windows, and a simple way to choose.

## Level I pass rates by window

These are the overall Level I pass rates from CFA Institute's published results for the four most recent windows:

| Exam window | Candidates | Overall pass rate |
|---|---|---|
| November 2025 | 26,752 | 43% |
| February 2026 | 24,006 | 45% |
| May 2026 | 31,566 | 39% |
| August 2026 | 28,145 | 43% |

CFA Institute's infographic puts the long-run Level I average at about 41%. So every recent window landed within a few points of it. February was the highest of the four and May the lowest, but that is four data points, and the gap between them is about what you'd expect from normal window-to-window movement.

It's tempting to conclude that May is the "hard" window because it had the most candidates and the lowest rate. CFA Institute doesn't make that claim, and nothing in the published numbers shows that the window itself causes the difference. Don't build a plan around it. For a deeper look at who passes and who doesn't, see our [Level 1 pass rate breakdown](/blog/cfa-level-1-pass-rate).

## What actually differs between windows

**Your study calendar.** This is the real variable. Most candidates need somewhere around 300 hours per level, which CFA Institute describes as what successful candidates report. At 12-15 hours a week, that's roughly five to six months. A window that lands on top of your busy season at work, finals, or a planned move is a bad window no matter what the statistics say. Our guide to [how many hours to study for CFA Level 1](/blog/how-many-hours-to-study-for-cfa-level-1) shows how to turn that into a weekly schedule.

**Registration price.** Early registration for Level I is $1,140 and standard registration is $1,490, a $350 difference. Each window has its own early deadline, and registering early means committing to a date months out. Full deadline dates are in our [CFA registration deadlines guide](/blog/cfa-exam-registration-deadlines).

**How fast you can move to Level II.** Level II is offered fewer times a year than Level I (three windows versus four), and you can't register for it until your Level I pass is posted. Results typically arrive several weeks after the window closes, so a candidate who passes often misses the early deadline for the next window and pays the standard fee. If you want to move through the program quickly, pick your Level I window with the Level II calendar in mind.

**Retake timing.** CFA Institute limits you to two attempts per level per calendar year, never in consecutive windows, with at least six months between attempts. That means if you take Level I in February and don't pass, your next opportunity is August; take it in May, and the retake is November. Knowing this in advance helps you decide how much cushion you want. The details are in our post on [how many times you can retake the CFA exam](/blog/how-many-times-can-you-retake-the-cfa-exam).

## Which window fits which candidate

**College students.** The window that falls after finals and gives you a clean block of study time is usually best. A summer window can work well if you're not interning full-time, and our post on [taking the CFA in college](/blog/can-you-take-the-cfa-in-college) covers the timing in more detail.

**Full-time professionals.** Count backward from the exam: if you can reliably find 12-15 hours a week, you need about 20-25 weeks. If your job has a predictable crunch (audit season, quarter-end, a deal), keep it out of the last eight weeks. Those weeks should be mock exams and review, not catching up. Our guide to [studying for the CFA while working full time](/blog/how-i-studied-for-the-cfa-while-working-full-time) goes deeper on this.

**Finance majors with a strong background.** You may be able to compress prep toward the shorter end, but a three-month plan only works if almost every week is productive. See the [three-month Level 1 plan](/blog/how-to-study-for-cfa-level-1-in-3-months) for what that actually requires.

**Anyone who's unsure.** Choose the later window. Starting with a registration you can't realistically prepare for is the most expensive mistake available here, especially because CFA Institute is ending paid deferrals starting with the February 2027 cycle, so a missed date now means forfeiting the fee. Our [deferral policy post](/blog/cfa-exam-deferral-policy) explains the change.

## The short version

Pass rates across the last four windows ran from 39% to 45%, which isn't enough of a spread to choose a window on. Choose by your calendar: the date that gives you roughly 300 hours, a quiet final eight weeks, and a fee you won't have to forfeit. Then protect it.
