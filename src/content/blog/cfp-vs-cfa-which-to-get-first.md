# CFP vs CFA: Which Should You Get First?

If you're managing money for institutions, take the CFA. If you're building financial plans for individuals and families, take the CFP. And if you genuinely might want both, take the CFA first — CFP Board's Accelerated Path lets charterholders skip almost the entire CFP coursework requirement, and there is no equivalent shortcut running the other direction.

That last point is the one most people miss, and it's worth a few thousand dollars. Here's the full comparison.

## What each credential is actually for

The CFA charter is an investment analysis credential. The curriculum is portfolio management, equity and fixed income valuation, derivatives, financial statement analysis, quantitative methods, and ethics. It's built for people who research securities, manage portfolios, or allocate capital — asset management, equity research, investment banking, risk, and increasingly private markets. There are more than 200,000 charterholders worldwide across 160+ countries.

The CFP marks are a financial planning credential. The curriculum is retirement planning, tax, estate, insurance, education funding, and the process of assembling those into a plan for a household. It's built for advisors who work directly with individual clients. More than 109,000 people in the U.S. hold CFP certification.

The overlap is real but narrower than it looks. Both cover investments and ethics. Neither substitutes for the other. A CFA charterholder who's never dealt with a client's estate plan is not equipped to be a financial planner, and a CFP professional who's never built a discounted cash flow model isn't going to get hired onto a research desk.

**So the first question isn't "which is harder." It's who your client is.** Institutions and portfolios point to the CFA. Households point to the CFP.

## The specs, side by side

| | CFA (Level I) | CFP |
|---|---|---|
| Governing body | CFA Institute | CFP Board |
| Exam structure | Three levels, each a separate exam | One exam |
| Format | 180 multiple-choice questions, two 135-minute sessions | 170 multiple-choice questions, two 3-hour sessions |
| Offered | 4x/year (Feb, May, Aug, Nov) | 3x/year (March, July, November) |
| 2026 exam fee | $1,140 early / $1,490 standard | $825 early / $925 standard / $1,025 late |
| Prerequisite coursework | None | Yes — through a CFP Board Registered Program |
| Experience required | 4,000 hours over a minimum of 36 months | 6,000 hours professional, or 4,000 hours apprenticeship |
| Degree required | Bachelor's (or equivalent work/education) | Bachelor's in any discipline |

## Pass rates: the numbers, honestly read

The February 2026 CFA Level I exam had a 45% pass rate among 24,006 candidates — above the 10-year average of 40%. First-time candidates passed at 50%.

The March 2026 CFP exam had a 67% pass rate among a record 4,391 candidates.

Those numbers make the CFP look roughly 50% easier, and that's misleading in both directions. The CFP has a gate in front of it: you must complete a CFP Board Registered Program's coursework before you can register. Everyone in that 67% has already been filtered and taught. The CFA has essentially no academic gate — anyone with a bachelor's degree and a passport can register for Level I, so the denominator includes a lot of underprepared candidates.

But the CFA is also, structurally, a much longer road. One CFP exam versus three CFA exams, each of which most candidates budget around 300 hours for. Passing all three levels on the first attempt is a multi-year commitment; we broke down the realistic time cost in our post on [how many hours to study for CFA Level 1](/blog/how-many-hours-to-study-for-cfa-level-1).

## The order actually matters (and saves money)

Here's the asymmetry that should drive your decision if you want both.

CFP Board runs an **Accelerated Path**. If you already hold the CFA charter — or a CPA, CIMA, CLU, ChFC, a law license, or a PhD in financial planning, business administration, or economics — you can bypass the bulk of the CFP education coursework and register for the CFP exam after completing just the Capstone course (or the Capstone Alternative) through a Registered Program.

That's a meaningful saving. Full CFP coursework through a Registered Program is a separate expense from the exam fee and varies widely by school; the Capstone alone is a fraction of it. You still have to self-report documentation of your qualifying credential plus proof you finished the Capstone.

Going the other way, holding the CFP does nothing to shorten the CFA Program. You still take all three levels.

**So: if both credentials are plausibly in your future, CFA first is the cheaper sequence.** If only one is, this doesn't apply — don't grind through three CFA levels as a coursework hack.

## Cost, all in

CFA registration for all three levels, registering early every time, runs $3,520 in 2026 fees. Retakes are full-price, so a couple of failed levels can push that past $5,000. We laid out every line item in our [CFA Level 1 cost breakdown](/blog/cfa-level-1-cost-and-fees).

CFP costs are structured differently: the exam fee is lower ($825–$1,025), but you add the coursework, a $250 certification application fee, and a $575 annual certification fee once you're certified. The recurring fee is the part people forget — the CFP is a subscription, not a one-time purchase.

Worth knowing before you budget: 68% of March 2026 CFP exam-takers reported some level of employer financial support toward certification. Ask your firm before you pay for anything.

## A practical decision rule

Pick the CFP if you want to advise individuals, you're at or heading to an RIA or wealth management firm, you want one exam rather than three, and you want to be credentialed within roughly a year.

Pick the CFA if you want an investment role, you're aiming at asset management or research, you're early enough in your career to absorb a multi-year program, or you want maximum optionality — the charter travels globally and, thanks to the Accelerated Path, leaves the CFP door open behind it.

If you're a student or career changer with no client base yet, the CFA-first argument is strongest. The CFP's 6,000-hour experience requirement means certification is gated on real client work anyway, so you can be studying for CFA Level I while you accumulate those hours. Notably, 47% of March 2026 CFP candidates were under 30 — this is increasingly an early-career credential, not a late-career one.
