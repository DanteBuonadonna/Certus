# CPA vs CFA Salary: What the Government Data Shows

The Bureau of Labor Statistics doesn't publish a salary for "CPAs" or "CFAs," because it tracks occupations, not credentials. The closest proxies tell a clear story: accountants and auditors had a median wage of $83,680 in May 2025, while financial analysts had a median of $103,570 and financial managers $166,570. The credential you earn mostly decides which of those doors opens, not what you're paid once you walk through.

Here's how to read those numbers honestly, and how to decide which exam is the better bet for your goals.

## The numbers, straight from the BLS

All figures below come from the Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook, which reports May 2025 wage data and projects job growth from 2025 to 2035.

| Occupation | Median pay (May 2025) | Projected growth, 2025–2035 |
|---|---|---|
| Accountants and auditors | $83,680 | 5% |
| Financial analysts | $103,570 | 7% |
| Personal financial advisors | $105,070 | 1% |
| Financial managers | $166,570 | 10% |

Every one of these typically requires a bachelor's degree to enter. Financial managers also typically need five or more years of experience in another business or financial occupation, which is a big part of why their pay sits so much higher. That's a reminder that seniority drives a lot of this table.

## Why "CPA vs CFA salary" is a slightly broken question

Three caveats matter before you compare credentials.

**Medians include everyone.** The accountants-and-auditors median covers people with and without a CPA license. The financial analyst median covers people with and without a CFA charter. Neither number isolates what the letters add. Anyone who quotes you a precise "CFA premium" or "CPA premium" is usually pulling from a survey with its own sample bias, so treat those figures skeptically.

**The credentials open different jobs.** The BLS notes that any accountant who files a report with the SEC must be a licensed CPA. The CPA is a license: in many accounting roles (public accounting, audit, tax, and SEC reporting), it's close to a requirement for advancement. The CFA, by contrast, is a designation that signals depth in investment analysis. It isn't a legal requirement for any job, but it's widely recognized in portfolio management, equity research, and asset management.

**Experience and employer beat credentials.** A senior accountant at a large firm and an entry-level analyst at a small shop live in different pay bands regardless of the letters after their names. Location, industry, and bonus structure (which is far bigger in investment roles than in accounting) all move total compensation more than a single credential does.

## So which pays more?

On the BLS proxies, financial analysts out-earn accountants and auditors at the median by roughly $20,000, and financial managers out-earn both by a wide margin. But the financial manager role is reached after years of experience, and plenty of those managers came up through accounting and hold a CPA. The BLS financial managers page specifically mentions CPA licensing, not the CFA.

The honest answer is that the higher-ceiling path depends on the job you want:

- **Public accounting, audit, tax, corporate accounting, or controller track:** the CPA is the standard, and in some roles it's effectively required.
- **Portfolio management, equity research, asset management, or investment banking-adjacent analysis:** the CFA is the credential recruiters recognize.
- **Corporate finance and FP&A:** either helps, and many people eventually have both.

## How to decide

Skip the salary arms race and ask three questions:

1. **What do I want to do all day?** Auditing and tax filings, or valuing companies and building portfolios? Pick the work first.
2. **What does my target employer ask for?** Read ten job postings for the role you want in five years. Count how many say CPA, CFA, or neither.
3. **Can I finish it?** A credential only pays if you complete it. Both are long, and pass rates are modest, so choose the one you'll stay motivated to finish.
