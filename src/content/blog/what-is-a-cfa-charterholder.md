# What Is a CFA Charterholder?

A CFA charterholder is someone who has passed all three levels of the CFA Program, completed at least 4,000 hours of qualifying investment work experience over a minimum of 36 months, and been accepted as a charterholder member of CFA Institute. Only people who have done all three of those things are allowed to put "CFA" after their name.

That last part trips up more people than you'd expect. Passing Level III does not make you a charterholder. Plenty of candidates finish the exams and then wait months or years before the letters are actually theirs, because the work experience or the membership application isn't done. The exams are the hard part, but they aren't the whole thing.

Here's exactly what the credential requires, straight from CFA Institute.

## The three requirements

### 1. Pass all three exam levels — plus the Practical Skills Modules

The CFA Program is a three-part exam sequence. Level I tests foundational terms, concepts, and formulas. Level II moves to analysis — you're given situations and asked to work through them. Level III is about integration and applying judgment to realistic scenarios.

Alongside the exams, CFA Institute now requires Practical Skills Modules (PSMs). These are separate applied units you complete at each level, with topic choices ranging from Financial Modeling and Analyst Skills to Python, Data Science, and AI. They're a genuine requirement, not a bonus, and candidates who plan only around exam dates sometimes forget them.

To enroll in the first place, you need one of three things: a completed bachelor's degree, qualifying undergraduate student status (your Level I exam window must fall within 23 months of your expected graduation month), or 4,000 hours of professional work experience and/or higher education accrued over at least three sequential years. You also need a valid international travel passport — no passport, no exam.

### 2. Log 4,000 hours of qualifying work experience

This is the requirement people underestimate. You need **at least 4,000 hours completed over a minimum of 36 months.** Both halves matter. You cannot compress 4,000 hours into 18 months of very long weeks and satisfy it; the clock has a floor of three years regardless of how many hours you pile up.

The hours can be earned before, during, or after you go through the CFA Program, which is why so many candidates who start the exams while already working in finance finish both requirements around the same time.

Not all finance work counts. To qualify, the experience must be *directly involved in the investment decision-making process, or produce a work product that informs or adds value to that process.* Full-time, part-time, and remote arrangements can all qualify. CFA Institute publishes a work experience self-assessment tool that's worth running early — much better to learn in year one that your role may not qualify than to discover it when you apply.

### 3. Become a CFA Institute member

The final step is applying for charterholder membership. You'll need to describe your current and prior job responsibilities in detail, and supply **three professional references** — or two, if one of them is an active regular member of the local society you're applying to.

Charterholder membership costs **USD 299 per year**, and most charterholders also pay separate dues to a local CFA society, which vary by city. This is an ongoing cost, not a one-time fee. Let your membership lapse and you lose the right to use the designation.

## How long does it actually take?

Longer than the exam calendar suggests, for two reasons.

First, the work experience floor is 36 months. Even a candidate who somehow cleared all three exams in eighteen months would still be waiting on the clock.

Second, pass rates compound. CFA Institute reported that 45% of the 24,006 candidates passed Level I in February 2026, and 39% of the 31,566 candidates passed in May 2026. Level II came in at 43% of 18,111 candidates in May 2026, and Level III at 50% of 11,269 candidates in February 2026.

Take those as rough anchors and do the arithmetic: if you had a coin-flip-ish shot at each of three independent levels, clearing all three consecutively without a single retake is a minority outcome. Most charterholders retake at least one level somewhere along the way. That isn't failure — it's the modal experience. Plan your timeline assuming one retake and you'll be pleasantly surprised if you don't need it.

## Is it worth it?

There are nearly 200,000 CFA Institute members and charterholders across 160 locations, so the charter is neither rare nor common — it's a recognized filter in asset management, equity research, and portfolio management specifically. If you want the honest version of that trade-off, we wrote a longer piece on [whether the CFA is worth it](/blog/is-the-cfa-worth-it).

The short version: the charter is most valuable when your target job actually lists it. In buy-side research and portfolio management it's close to table stakes. In investment banking, corporate finance, or fintech, it's a nice-to-have that rarely moves a hiring decision on its own.
