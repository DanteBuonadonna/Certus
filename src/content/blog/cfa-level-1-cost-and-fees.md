# CFA Level 1 Cost in 2026: Every Fee, Broken Down

Registering for CFA Level 1 in 2026 costs $1,140 if you catch the early registration deadline, or $1,490 if you register at the standard rate. There's no longer a separate enrollment fee — CFA Institute eliminated the one-time $350 enrollment fee starting with the February 2026 exams, raising registration fees at the same time.

That's the direct answer. But the registration fee is only part of what the CFA charter actually costs you, and the 2026 pricing changes confused a lot of candidates. Here's the full picture.

## What changed in 2026

For years, first-time Level 1 candidates paid two fees: a one-time $350 enrollment fee plus the exam registration fee. As of late April 2025, CFA Institute scrapped the enrollment fee for exams from February 2026 onward — but raised registration fees by $150 per level to compensate.

The net effect for a brand-new candidate: you save about $50 across the whole program compared to 2025 pricing. For anyone retaking an exam (who already paid the enrollment fee years ago), it's a straight price increase.

## 2026 CFA exam fees by level

| Exam | Early registration | Standard registration |
|------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Level 1 | $1,140 | $1,490 |
| Level 2 | $1,140 | $1,490 |
| Level 3 | $1,240 | $1,590 |

Registering early saves you $350 per level — $1,050 across the program. There is no scenario where waiting for the standard deadline gets you anything except a bigger bill, so if you know you're taking the exam, register the day you decide.

Two other CFA Institute fees worth knowing about: rescheduling your exam appointment within your window costs $250, and if you want printed curriculum books instead of the included e-books, that's an extra $250 per level plus shipping. The digital curriculum comes free with your registration.

One logistical item that surprises people: you need a valid international travel passport just to register. If yours is expired, factor in the renewal fee and processing time before the registration deadline.

## The total cost of all three levels

If you pass every level on your first attempt and always register early, your registration fees for the full program total $3,520 ($1,140 + $1,140 + $1,240). Always paying the standard rate pushes that to $4,570.

Those are the best-case numbers. The thing that actually blows up CFA budgets is retakes. Every retake is a full new registration fee — there's no discount for trying again — so a single failed level adds $1,140 to $1,590 to your total. Fail one exam per level and your registration costs alone can double. CFA Institute also requires you to wait at least six months between attempts, with a maximum of two attempts per calendar year, so a failed exam costs you time as well as money.

This is worth pausing on: the cheapest possible path through the CFA Program is passing on the first try. Everything else in your budget should serve that goal.

## What prep actually costs (and what it doesn't have to)

Here's where candidate budgets diverge wildly. The registration fee already includes the complete official curriculum as e-books, so the only truly required extra purchase is an approved calculator — the Texas Instruments BA II Plus runs about $35–$50, and the HP 12C about $75.

Everything beyond that is optional. Third-party prep courses from providers like Kaplan Schweser, UWorld, and AnalystPrep run roughly $499 to $1,499 per level depending on the package. Many candidates budget around $800 per level for materials, which means prep courses can easily cost more than the exam itself across three levels.

## Ways to cut the cost

A few levers actually move the number:

**Register early, every time.** It's $350 per level, and it also forces you to commit to a study timeline sooner — which correlates with passing. Candidates who cram late are the ones funding retake fees. (For planning purposes: most candidates need roughly 300 hours per level. Our breakdown of [how many hours to study for CFA Level 1](/blog/how-many-hours-to-study-for-cfa-level-1) has the details.)

**Ask your employer.** Many finance employers reimburse exam fees, prep materials, or both. Ask before you register — some firms only reimburse fees paid after approval.

**Apply for a CFA Institute scholarship.** The needs-based Access Scholarship reduces the registration fee to $400, and the Student Scholarship (for students at affiliated universities) cuts it to $600. If you might qualify, apply before registering.

**Don't pay $1,000+ to avoid reading the curriculum.** Spend money on practice, not on a second copy of content you already own.

## Budget for after you pass, too

The spending doesn't fully stop at Level 3. Using the CFA designation requires regular membership in CFA Institute, which costs $299 per year, and most charterholders also join a local CFA society with its own dues — typically somewhere in the $100–$275 range depending on the society. It's a future cost, not part of your exam budget, but it belongs in the "is this worth it" math. (We tackled that bigger question head-on in [Is the CFA worth it?](/blog/is-the-cfa-worth-it))

For a first-time 2026 candidate who registers early, CFA Level 1 costs $1,140 in fees, about $40 for a calculator, and as much or as little as you choose to spend on prep.
