# Can You Take the CFA in College? The Student Rules

Yes. You can take CFA Level I while you're still an undergraduate, as long as the exam window you pick is 23 months or less before the month you graduate. In practice, that means most students become eligible partway through junior year.

You don't need a degree to enroll, and you don't need a finance major. But the rules get stricter at each level, and a lot of students find out about the Level II and Level III cutoffs only after they've built a plan that doesn't work. Here's the full picture.

## The student eligibility rules, level by level

CFA Institute lets you enroll in the program in one of three ways: you hold a bachelor's degree, you're an undergraduate student, or you have 4,000 hours of combined work experience and/or higher education built up over at least three sequential years. Most college candidates use the second route, and it comes with a timing rule for each level:

| Exam | Undergraduate timing rule |
|---|---|
| Level I | Exam window must be **23 months or less** before your graduation month |
| Level II | You must be **within 11 months** of graduation |
| Level III | You must have **completed your bachelor's degree** (or have 4,000 hours of professional work experience) |

A few other requirements apply to everyone. You need a valid international travel passport to register and to take the exam, and the exams are offered only in English.

This is newer than a lot of advice online suggests. Until late 2022, students had to be in their final year to register for Level I. CFA Institute extended eligibility by a full year effective November 1, 2022, explicitly so students could use Level I as a signal to employers when they're competing for internships. If an older forum post tells you that you have to wait until senior year, it's out of date.

## Working out your own dates

The rules are written in months, so the easiest way to apply them is to count backward from your graduation month. Take a student who graduates in **May 2028**:

- **Level I:** 23 months before May 2028 is June 2026. Any Level I window from June 2026 onward qualifies, so the February 2027 and May 2027 windows are both open to this student right now.
- **Level II:** 11 months before May 2028 is June 2027. The August 2027 window qualifies; May 2027 does not.
- **Level III:** The degree has to be finished first, so the earliest option is the first Level III window after May 2028.

Now run the same math for a sophomore graduating in **May 2029**. Twenty-three months back is June 2027, so the May 2027 window is off-limits (it's 24 months out), but August 2027 works. One month makes the difference, so count carefully before you pay anything.

Level I runs four times a year (February, May, August, and November), so you'll usually have several eligible windows to pick from. Our [CFA exam registration deadlines guide](/blog/cfa-exam-registration-deadlines) has the exact early and final dates for each upcoming window.

## Can you finish the CFA before you graduate?

No. Level III requires a completed degree (or the 4,000-hour work route), so you can't take all three exams as a student. What you *can* do is finish Level I, and potentially Level II, before graduation.

Even after you pass Level III, you aren't a charterholder until you've also logged at least 4,000 hours of qualifying work experience over a minimum of 36 months and been admitted as a member. We cover that checklist in [what is a CFA charterholder](/blog/what-is-a-cfa-charterholder).

## What it costs a student

Registration is the same price for students as for everyone else: **$1,140** at the early deadline or **$1,490** at the standard deadline for Level I. The official curriculum comes as e-books with your registration, and the only other required purchase is an approved calculator.

There are two discounts worth checking:

- **Student Scholarship.** If your school is part of CFA Institute's University Affiliation Program, you can apply to cut your registration fee to **$600**, and since March 4, 2026 that rate includes the Premium package with the Practice Pack. You only need to attend an affiliated school; you don't have to be in a specific affiliated degree program. Applications are accepted from September 1 through July 15, and your university reviews them.
- **Access Scholarship.** This is need-based and cuts registration to **$400**. Details and application windows are in our [CFA Access Scholarship guide](/blog/cfa-access-scholarship).

One warning that applies to both: if you're awarded a scholarship and don't register, schedule, and show up for the exam, you lose eligibility for future CFA Institute scholarships. Only apply for a window you're actually going to take.

## Is it worth taking Level I in college?

It can be, but go in clear-eyed about the workload. CFA Institute's own guidance is about 300 hours of study per level, which is roughly a semester-long course's worth of effort stacked on top of your actual courses. Students tend to manage it best in one of two ways:

1. **Use a lighter semester or a summer.** If your Level I window comes a few months after summer break, you can do most of your heavy studying while you have no classes.
2. **Start early and go slow.** Spreading 300 hours over five or six months works out to about 12 to 15 hours a week, which is doable alongside a normal course load if you're consistent.

The case for doing it is mostly about timing. Passing Level I before recruiting season gives you something concrete to point to on a resume, and much of the Level I curriculum overlaps with what you're already learning in finance, accounting, and economics courses. If you're on the fence, our [is the CFA worth it](/blog/is-the-cfa-worth-it) breakdown covers the bigger career math.

The case against is also real: a failed attempt costs you the full registration fee, and you'll have to wait at least six months before you can try that level again. If midterms and a recruiting push land in the same month as your exam window, waiting one window is often the smarter move.
