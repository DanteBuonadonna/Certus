# Best Free CFA Study Resources (2026)

The best free CFA study resources are the ones already included with your registration — the full curriculum, 1,000+ practice questions, and two official mock exams inside CFA Institute's Learning Ecosystem — plus a handful of genuinely free third-party tools like practice mocks, formula guides, and community forums. Before you spend $350–$1,500 on a prep course, know exactly what you can get for $0.

That matters more in 2026 than ever, because the exam itself got more expensive. CFA Institute eliminated the one-time $350 enrollment fee starting with the February 2026 exams, but raised registration fees at the same time: Level 1 now costs $1,140 with early registration or $1,490 at the standard deadline. When the exam alone runs four figures, keeping prep costs low isn't cheap — it's rational.

Here's what's actually free, what it's good for, and where the gaps are.

## 1. The CFA Institute Learning Ecosystem (included with registration)

This is the resource most candidates underuse. When you register for the exam, you get access to the Learning Ecosystem (LES), which includes:

- **The full official curriculum** for your level. Every testable learning outcome statement comes from here. Third-party providers summarize this material; the LES *is* the material.
- **1,000+ practice questions**, written by the same people who write the actual exam questions.
- **Two official mock exams**, released roughly 60 days before your exam window.
- A study planner and curriculum-based flashcards.

Two things the LES does especially well: **Ethics** and **question style**. Ethics is graded against CFA Institute's own framing, so third-party summaries of the Code and Standards are always a step removed from the source — read the official curriculum for Ethics even if you use other materials for everything else. And official practice questions are the truest preview of how the real exam phrases things.

The honest downside: the curriculum is long and dry. Most candidates who rely on it exclusively struggle to get through the reading in time, which is why the prep-course industry exists. The fix doesn't have to cost $1,000, though — more on that below.

## 2. Free full-length mock exams

Mocks are where free resources get thin. CFA Institute gives you two official mocks, but they arrive only ~60 days before your window — and most candidates want to benchmark themselves much earlier than that.

No email capture, no credit card, no "free trial" that converts to a charge. Take it early in your prep to find your weak topics, then save the official CFA Institute mocks for the final two months when they're released.

A sensible free mock sequence looks like this: take the Certus mock 4–6 months out to get a baseline, drill your weakest topic areas, then take the two official mocks in the last 60 days under timed, exam-day conditions.

## 3. Free question practice beyond the LES

Several established providers offer limited free tiers or trials of their question banks — AnalystPrep, Kaplan Schweser, and UWorld all have some form of free sample access, and Kaplan's free "question of the day" email is a low-effort way to keep concepts warm. These are marketing funnels, so expect the free portion to be a small slice of the paid product, but a small slice of a good question bank still beats re-reading notes.

The thing to remember about question practice: volume and review matter more than source. Whether a question came from a $1,000 course or a free tier, what moves your score is doing it under time pressure and understanding *why* the wrong answers are wrong.

## 4. Free video content

YouTube has real, substantive CFA content — full topic lectures, formula walkthroughs, and exam-technique videos from established instructors and prep providers. Quality varies, and no free video library covers the full curriculum systematically, but for a topic you're stuck on (pension accounting, currency translation, swap valuation), a good 20-minute video explanation is often the fastest unstick available.

Use video as a supplement, not a spine. Watching lectures feels productive but tests poorly — the pass-rate data consistently rewards active practice over passive review.

## 5. Free community: r/CFA and study forums

The r/CFA subreddit is the largest free CFA community and it's legitimately useful: exam-day threads, provider comparisons, score post-mortems, and honest answers about how much time topics actually take. The 2025 results threads, for example, surfaced a pattern CFA Institute's own data confirms — Level 1 pass rates ran 43–45% across 2025, above the 10-year average of 41%, and first-time candidates who kept their original exam date passed at meaningfully higher rates than those who deferred. Staying on schedule is one of the strongest predictors of passing.

Forums are best for calibration ("is my mock score normal at this stage?") and worst for anxiety (someone is always claiming they studied 600 hours and failed). Take both with salt.
