# Can You Defer the CFA Exam? 2026–2027 Deferral Rules

Yes, but only for a little while longer. If you're registered for the November 2026 window, you can still buy a one-time $449 paid deferral between October 27 and November 24, 2026; starting with the February 2027 exams, CFA Institute is eliminating paid deferrals entirely, so a candidate who doesn't show up simply forfeits the registration fee.

Emergency deferrals for documented hardships still exist after the change, but the bar is narrow. Here's how both options work, what they cost, and how to decide whether deferring actually makes sense.

## The two kinds of CFA deferral

CFA Institute runs two separate deferral tracks, and they have different rules, fees, and deadlines.

**Paid deferral ($449).** No reason required. You pay the fee, your current registration and scheduled appointment are canceled immediately, and you get a voucher code to register for a later exam within 12 months. It's the "life got in the way" option.

**Emergency deferral ($100 processing fee).** Reserved for specific hardships you can document. If CFA Institute approves your request, you pay a $100 processing fee and get the same 12-month window to take a later exam. If your exam was rescheduled by Prometric within 30 days of the window start and you can't find an acceptable new appointment, there's no processing fee at all.

A third option people confuse with deferral: **rescheduling within the same window**. If you just need a different day or test center inside your current window, that costs $250 and doesn't touch your registration. It has its own rescheduling cutoff before each window, after which you can't move at all.

## November 2026 deadlines

If you're registered for November 2026, these are the dates that matter, per CFA Institute's policy pages:

| Option | Request window | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| Paid deferral | Oct 27 – Nov 24, 2026 (opens 10:00 AM ET) | $449 |
| Emergency deferral | Nov 6 – Nov 24, 2026 (opens 10:00 AM ET) | $100 if approved |

For a November 2026 paid deferral, Level I candidates can move to the February, May, August, or November 2027 windows. Level II candidates can move to May, August, or November 2027. There's no Level III window in November, so that column doesn't apply.

A few rules apply to every paid deferral:

- **It's binding.** Once you pay, your appointment is canceled and you can't change your mind. No refunds.
- **One per registration.** You can't defer the deferred exam. If plans change again, you pay for a new registration.
- **Registration must still be open** for the window you want. If that window's deadline has passed, you'll have to choose a later one.
- **Your Learning Ecosystem progress doesn't carry over.** Each exam window uses its own set of courses, so you start fresh in the new one.

## What changes in February 2027

CFA Institute announced the change in March 2026. Beginning with the February 2027 exam cycle:

- **Paid deferrals are gone.** If you're registered and choose not to take your exam, you forfeit the registration fee and must re-register at whatever rate is available for a later window.
- **Pregnancy is no longer a qualifying reason for an emergency deferral**, except in cases of life-threatening complications. CFA Institute says it can help pregnant candidates with special arrangements on exam day instead.

Paid deferrals were introduced during COVID, when test center closures and travel restrictions made case-by-case review impractical. CFA Institute's stated reasons for ending them are that testing has normalized, there are now more windows and test centers, and its own data consistently shows that candidates who defer pass at noticeably lower rates than first-time takers who stick to their original date.

That last point is worth taking seriously. Deferring rarely means "the same preparation, just later." For a lot of people it means losing momentum, then cramming again months later.

## Who qualifies for an emergency deferral

For exams before February 2027, CFA Institute's emergency policy covers:

- Life-threatening illness of the candidate or an immediate family member
- Death of an immediate family member
- Mandatory military deployment (candidate only) during the exam window
- Pregnancy (candidate only, one per level; this reason goes away in February 2027 except for life-threatening complications)
- Prometric rescheduling your exam within 30 days of the window start when you can't find another acceptable date

"Immediate family" means a parent, sibling, spouse or domestic partner, or child. Grandparents don't count.

You must submit documentation. Review takes up to two weeks, and if you're approved you have 7 days to pay the processing fee or the deferral is voided. Approval isn't guaranteed, and if a request is denied after your appointment has already passed, you may end up paying for a new registration.

One warning CFA Institute states plainly: it has received numerous false emergency deferral requests, and falsified documentation gets referred to Professional Conduct, with sanctions up to being barred from the exam. If you don't qualify, don't try to make it fit.

## Should you defer? A quick way to decide

Run the numbers first. For November 2026 candidates, the choice is $449 now versus forfeiting what you paid and re-registering later. For the 2027 windows, CFA Institute lists Level I and II registration at $1,140 early and $1,490 standard, and Level III at $1,240 early and $1,590 standard. From February 2027 on, a no-show means paying that again in full.

Then be honest about readiness. A useful test, four to six weeks out:

1. **Take a full-length, timed mock.** Not a topic quiz. The whole thing, in one sitting.
2. **Look at your score and your trend**, not just your feelings. If you're in striking distance and improving, the remaining weeks are usually enough to close the gap, especially in high-weight topics.
3. **Find the real constraint.** If it's time (a new job, a family emergency), deferring may be right. If it's nerves, deferring usually doesn't fix it; more timed practice does.

If you're far off with no realistic way to catch up, a November 2026 paid deferral is the last chance to buy a cheap second shot. After that, the safer move is to register only for a window you're confident you can prepare for. If you do defer, protect yourself from the pattern CFA Institute's data points to: set a fixed weekly study schedule right away rather than "restarting later," and keep doing practice questions so you don't lose what you already know.
