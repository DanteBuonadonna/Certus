# CFP Certification Requirements: The 4 E's Explained

To earn CFP certification you must meet four requirements set by CFP Board, known as the "4 E's": **Education** (approved coursework plus a bachelor's degree), **Examination** (pass the CFP exam), **Experience** (6,000 hours, or 4,000 hours through the apprenticeship pathway), and **Ethics** (sign the Ethics Declaration and pass a background check). CFP Board puts the typical length of the whole process at 18–24 months, though the order is more flexible than most people expect.

Below is what each requirement actually involves, which ones must come first, and the deadlines that trip candidates up.

## The 4 E's at a glance

| Requirement | What you need | When it must be done |
|---|---|---|
| Education: coursework | Complete a CFP Board Registered Program | **Before** you can take the exam |
| Education: degree | Bachelor's degree or higher, any major, from an accredited school | Before, or within 5 years after, passing the exam |
| Exam | Pass the 170-question CFP exam | After coursework is verified |
| Experience | 6,000 hours (Standard) or 4,000 hours (Apprenticeship) | Within 10 years before or 5 years after passing the exam |
| Ethics | Ethics Declaration + background check | Final step |

The only hard sequencing rule is that coursework comes before the exam. Your degree and your experience can be finished afterward, which is why plenty of people take the exam while still building their hours.

## 1. Education: coursework and a degree

The education requirement has two parts.

**Coursework.** You must complete college-level financial planning coursework through a CFP Board Registered Program. The curriculum covers professional conduct and regulation, general principles of financial planning, risk management and insurance, investment planning, tax planning, retirement savings and income planning, estate planning, and the psychology of financial planning. It ends with a capstone course in financial plan development. CFP Board says the coursework takes an average of 12–18 months.

Proof that you've completed it has to reach CFP Board by the **education verification deadline** for the exam window you want. For the March 2027 window, that deadline is February 23, 2027.

**Bachelor's degree.** You need a bachelor's degree or higher, in any field, from an accredited college or university. It doesn't have to be in finance. You can still be finishing it when you take the exam: you have five years after passing to submit the transcript.

### The Accelerated Path (if you already hold a credential)

If you already hold certain credentials, CFP Board lets you skip most of the coursework through its Accelerated Path. Qualifying credentials include:

- CPA
- CFA charter
- Licensed attorney
- ChFC or CLU
- CIMA
- Ph.D. in financial planning, finance, business administration, or economics
- Doctor of Business Administration
- CFP certification from an FPSB affiliate outside the U.S.

You still have to complete the capstone course (or the Capstone Alternative), pass the exam, finish your experience hours, and submit your degree transcript. For CFA charterholders, this is the main reason the CFP is often treated as an add-on rather than a second multi-year project. ## 2. Examination: the CFP exam

The CFP exam has 170 multiple-choice questions, split into two 3-hour sessions on a single day. You'll see stand-alone questions, scenario-based questions, and questions tied to case studies. It's offered during three 8-day testing windows a year, in March, July, and November, and you can test at a Prometric center or request remote proctoring.

**Exam fees:**

- Early bird: $825 (until six weeks before the registration deadline)
- Standard: $925
- Late (final week of registration): $1,025

**Upcoming windows:**

- October 29 – November 5, 2026 (late registration closes October 13, 2026)
- March 16–23, 2027 (registration opens October 21, 2026)
- July 13–20, 2027 (registration opens February 17, 2027)

CFP Board recommends registering at least 60 days ahead, since Prometric appointments are first come, first served. On pass rates, CFP Board reported that roughly 67% of first-time takers passed in 2019. ## 3. Experience: 6,000 or 4,000 hours

This is the requirement that takes the longest for most career-changers, and the one with the most fine print.

**Standard Pathway: 6,000 hours.** Your work has to relate to at least one of the seven elements of the financial planning process, such as understanding a client's circumstances, developing recommendations, or monitoring progress. You can count hours from working directly with clients, supporting or supervising planners, a qualifying internship or the FPA residency program, or teaching college-level financial planning courses.

**Apprenticeship Pathway: 4,000 hours.** You get a shorter route if you meet three stricter conditions:

1. Your work covers **all seven** elements of the planning process.
2. You're primarily engaging with individual clients for financial planning.
3. You work under the **direct supervision of a CFP professional**, who must verify and attest to your hours.

**Rules that catch people out:**

- **Hours are capped at 40 a week.** Overtime doesn't speed things up. At 40 qualifying hours a week, 6,000 hours is about three years of full-time work, and 4,000 is about two.
- **Not every hour counts.** Corporate finance, marketing, practice management, software development, training, and admin work don't count, even at a planning firm.
- **Family members aren't clients.** Planning for immediate relatives, as FINRA Rule 3240 defines them, doesn't count.
- **You can't mix pathways.** Every qualifying entry counts toward the 6,000-hour standard, and entries that also meet the apprenticeship criteria count toward both totals at once. You're done when you cross either line first.

**The timing window.** Experience must fall within 10 years before or 5 years after you pass the exam. If you haven't finished your experience and your degree within five years of passing, you have to retake the exam. You can request a one-time three-year extension, but it's granted case by case and must be requested at least 60 days before your deadline.

There's also a smaller catch: if your experience isn't finished within 12 months of passing the exam, you start accruing continuing education at 1.25 hours a month, and you have to complete it before you can be certified.

## 4. Ethics: the final step

When everything else is done, you sign CFP Board's Ethics Declaration, which commits you to its standards of conduct, including acting as a fiduciary when giving financial advice. CFP Board also runs a background check. Certain past conduct can disqualify you under its Fitness Standards, so if anything in your history concerns you, review those standards before investing in coursework.

## A realistic timeline

Here's how a typical candidate's path might look:

1. **Months 0–15:** Complete the coursework (CFP Board's average is 12–18 months), ideally while already working in a planning role so your hours start adding up.
2. **Months 15–18:** Register early-bird for the next window and study for the exam.
3. **After passing:** Finish any remaining experience hours (try to do it within 12 months to avoid the pre-certification CE), submit your degree if needed, then complete the ethics step.

If you already hold an Accelerated Path credential, the coursework phase shrinks to the capstone, and the experience requirement usually becomes the deciding factor in your timeline.
