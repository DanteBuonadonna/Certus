# CFA Exam Day: What to Expect (2026 Walkthrough)

Here's the short version: you'll take the CFA exam at a proctored Prometric test center, you must arrive 30 minutes before your appointment with a valid passport and an approved calculator, and the Level I exam runs two 135-minute sessions of 90 questions each with an optional break in between. Everything else — the lockers, the check-in pat-down, the on-screen timer — is detail, but it's exactly the kind of detail that rattles people who didn't know it was coming. This guide walks through the whole day so nothing surprises you.

Months of prep can get undermined by a bad morning. Knowing the logistics cold means the only thing you're thinking about at 8 a.m. is the material.

## Before you leave the house

Two items are non-negotiable, and forgetting either one ends your exam day before it starts:

**Your passport.** CFA Institute accepts one form of identification: a valid international travel passport. Not a driver's license, not a national ID card. If your passport is expired or the name doesn't match your registration, you will not test. Check this the week you register, not the week of the exam.

**An approved calculator.** Only two models are allowed: the Texas Instruments BA II Plus (including the Professional edition) or the Hewlett-Packard 12C (including the Platinum and anniversary editions). Proctors inspect your calculator at check-in. Bring the one you actually practiced on — exam day is not the time to relearn keystrokes.

You're also allowed to bring water in a clear bottle with no label, extra layers of clothing (test center temperatures are a genuine wildcard), and basic personal care items. Food stays in your locker. Almost everything else — phone, watch, notes, your own scratch paper — gets left in the designated personal belongings area.

## Check-in: what actually happens

Arrive 30 minutes before your scheduled appointment. This isn't a suggestion; if you show up late, you can be refused admission. Build in buffer for traffic, parking, and finding the right suite in an office building you've never visited.

At check-in, staff verify your passport, inspect your calculator, and have you stow your belongings. Expect security to be thorough — test centers monitor for cheating and many use video recording throughout the facility. It can feel a little airport-like. That's normal, and it moves quickly.

You'll be walked to a workstation with a computer and provided note-taking materials. Your appointment clock doesn't start until you're seated and your exam launches, so a short wait in the lobby doesn't eat into your testing time.

## The structure of the day

Once you're seated, the Level I exam day follows a fixed sequence, per [CFA Institute's exam day guide](https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa-program/candidate-resources/exam-day-preparation-guide):

- Introduction, candidate pledge, and software tutorial: 19 minutes
- Session 1: 90 questions, 135 minutes
- Optional break: about 30 minutes
- Session 2: 90 questions, 135 minutes
- A short survey, then you're done

That's 180 multiple-choice questions total, each with three answer choices (A, B, or C), and no penalty for wrong answers — so never leave a question blank. Levels II and III follow the same shape but with two 132-minute sessions and vignette-based questions. A pacing note: 135 minutes for 90 questions works out to 90 seconds per question. That sounds tight, but many questions — especially Ethics and definitional ones — take 30 seconds, which banks time for the calculation-heavy ones. ## The break

The break between sessions is optional, but take it. Four and a half hours of screen time is a long haul, and the second session is where fatigue errors creep in. Stand up, eat something from your locker, use the restroom, and reset. One caution: once you finish Session 1, you cannot go back to it. Review any flagged questions before you close out the first session.

## During the exam

You work at your own pace within each session. The software lets you flag questions and return to them (within the same session only). If something goes wrong — a screen freeze, a noisy neighbor, a broken calculator — raise your hand and a proctor will come to you. CFA Institute says the exam is designed to absorb minor disruptions, so don't panic over a two-minute hiccup.

One thing that surprises first-timers: the test center is not silent. Other candidates are taking different exams on different schedules, so people come and go. Earplugs are typically available; consider using them if you're noise-sensitive.

## After you finish

You won't get a score on screen. CFA Institute notifies candidates of results within roughly 5 to 9 weeks after the exam window closes.

There's one post-exam requirement that catches people off guard: the Practical Skills Module (PSM). Every candidate must complete at least one PSM — a 10–20 hour online module on topics like Python or analyst skills — before their result will be released. It's not part of the exam and it isn't graded like one, but if you haven't finished it by results day, your result gets withheld until you do. Knock it out in the weeks right after the exam while you're waiting anyway.

## The night before: keep it boring

The best exam-day preparation is unremarkable: confirm your appointment time and test center address, lay out your passport and calculator (with fresh batteries), plan to arrive early, and stop studying by evening. Cramming the night before a 180-question exam buys you almost nothing; sleep buys you a sharper second session.
