# CFA Level 3 Pass Rate: 2026 Data and What It Means

The CFA Level 3 pass rate was **50% in February 2026**, the most recent Level III window with published results. CFA Institute puts the 10-year average (2017–2026) at **51%**, which makes Level III the level with the highest pass rate of the three. It's still a coin flip for a group of candidates who have already passed two very hard exams.

If you took Level III in August 2026, your results are scheduled for **October 20, 2026**, according to CFA Institute. Here's how to read the numbers while you wait, or while you plan your attempt.

## Every recent Level III window

All figures below come from CFA Institute's official historical results table.

| Exam window | Candidates | Pass rate |
|---|---|---|
| February 2026 | 11,269 | 50% |
| August 2025 | 14,538 | 50% |
| February 2025 | 11,131 | 49% |
| August 2024 | 16,947 | 48% |
| February 2024 | 13,619 | 49% |
| August 2023 | 16,035 | 47% |
| February 2023 | 14,858 | 48% |
| August 2022 | 10,376 | 48% |
| February 2022 | 11,699 | 44% |

Two things stand out. First, the numbers are remarkably stable: every window since 2023 has landed between 47% and 50%. Unlike Level I, which has swung widely since the move to computer-based testing, Level III has been predictable. Second, results are a little lower than before the pandemic. The annual Level III pass rates for 2017, 2018, and 2019 were 54%, 56%, and 56%.

## How Level 3 compares with Levels 1 and 2

| Level | 10-year average pass rate (2017–2026) |
|---|---|
| Level I | 40% |
| Level II | 45% |
| Level III | 51% |

That ranking can mislead you. Level III doesn't have a higher pass rate because it's the easiest exam. It's higher because of who takes it. Everyone in the room has already passed Levels I and II, which filters out a large share of the people who started the program. You're competing against a much stronger pool of candidates, and roughly half of them still don't pass.

## The number that matters most: first-timers vs. deferrers

The headline rate hides a much bigger gap. In the February 2026 window, CFA Institute reported:

- **First-time Level III candidates:** 59% pass rate
- **Candidates with at least one deferral behind them:** 34% pass rate

That's a 25-point difference. It lines up with what CFA Institute saw at Level I in August 2026, where first-timers passed at 49% and candidates who had deferred passed at 29%.

We can't tell you *why* from the data alone. Deferring is often a sign that life got in the way of studying, and some of that gap likely reflects preparation rather than the deferral itself. Either way, the practical takeaway is the same: **pick a window you can actually study for, then show up.**

That matters more than it used to. Beginning with the February 2027 exam cycle, CFA Institute is eliminating paid deferrals. If you don't take your scheduled exam, you forfeit the registration fee and have to register again. ## Why Level 3 trips people up

Level III tests a different skill from the first two levels. CFA Institute describes it as "integrate and apply," and the format is built to test that.

**Half the exam is essays.** The Level III exam has 11 item sets and 11 constructed response (essay) sets, split across two 132-minute sessions. Twenty of those 22 sets are scored, and each is worth 12 points. Candidates who have spent two levels picking between three answer choices often struggle when they have to write the answer themselves.

**Command words decide your score.** In essay questions, the key verb (**Determine**, **Justify**, **Explain**, **Discuss**) is bolded, and each one expects a specific kind of answer. CFA Institute also notes that when a question asks for a specific number of responses, graders read only up to that number, in the order you wrote them. Adding a third reason when the question asks for two earns you nothing.

**Calculations are graded differently.** CFA Institute says a correct number typed into the essay box by itself gets full credit. Showing your work isn't required, but it's the only way to earn partial credit if your final number is wrong. When you're unsure of a calculation, write out the steps.

**The content is portfolio management, top to bottom.** The core curriculum covers Asset Allocation (15–20%), Portfolio Construction (15–20%), Derivatives and Risk Management (10–15%), Ethical and Professional Standards (10–15%), and Performance Measurement (5–10%). Your chosen pathway accounts for the remaining 30–35%. If you haven't picked one yet, our guide to [CFA Level 3 pathways](/blog/cfa-level-3-pathways-which-to-choose) walks through the choice.

## What the pass rate means for your prep

A roughly 50% pass rate among experienced candidates tells you that knowing the material isn't enough. You have to deliver it in the exam's format and under time pressure. A few practical implications:

1. **Practice essays early, not in the final month.** Write out full answers to past-style constructed response questions under time limits. Writing is its own skill, and it takes weeks to build.
2. **Grade yourself strictly.** Compare your answer to the guideline answer line by line. Did you answer the command word? Did you give exactly the number of reasons requested?
3. **Budget your time by points.** Every set is worth 12 points, so no single vignette deserves a disproportionate share of a 132-minute session.
4. **Put in the hours.** CFA Institute reports that successful candidates spend over 300 hours on average preparing for each level. Being a Level III candidate doesn't mean you can get by with less.
5. **Remember the Practical Skills Module.** You have to complete a PSM at Level III to receive your exam result, so don't leave it for the last week.
