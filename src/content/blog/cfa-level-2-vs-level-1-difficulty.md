# CFA Level 2 vs Level 1: How Much Harder Is It?

Level 2 is harder than Level 1 in kind, not in pass rate — the questions go deeper, but the pass rate is actually a little *higher*. CFA Institute's own 10-year averages put Level 1 at 41% and Level 2 at 45%, and the most recent sittings follow the same pattern: 39% passed Level 1 in May 2026, while 43% passed Level 2.

That statistic surprises people, and it's also the most misread number in the whole CFA Program. Here's what's really going on, and what changes about your prep.

## The pass rate is higher because the field is filtered

Everyone taking Level 2 already passed Level 1. That's a self-selected group — they've proven they can grind through 300+ hours of curriculum and show up on exam day. So a 45% pass rate among Level 2 candidates is not the same bar as a 41% pass rate among a Level 1 field that includes a lot of people who registered in January and opened the curriculum in April.

Read it this way instead: at Level 1, roughly six in ten people fail. At Level 2, roughly six in ten of the people who *already beat that* pass. The exam did not get easier. The room got stronger.

## What actually changes: the format

This is the single biggest difference, and it's structural.

**Level 1** is 180 standalone multiple-choice questions across two 135-minute sessions (4.5 hours total). Each question is self-contained. Miss one, you lose one point, and the next question starts fresh.

**Level 2** is 22 item sets — vignettes, each followed by 4 multiple-choice questions — for 88 questions total, split into two 132-minute sessions (4 hours and 24 minutes). Twenty of those item sets are scored; two are being trialed and don't count, and you won't know which.

The vignette is a page or two of case material: a company's financials, an analyst's notes, a portfolio manager's assumptions, a set of quotes. You can't answer the questions without going back into it. That changes the math on your time:

- **Level 1:** 270 minutes ÷ 180 questions = **90 seconds per question**
- **Level 2:** 264 minutes ÷ 22 item sets = **12 minutes per item set** — read the vignette, then answer 4 questions in what's left

Twelve minutes sounds generous until you've spent five of them figuring out which numbers in the vignette matter. And because four questions hang off one scenario, a single misreading can cost you four points instead of one. That compounding is the real difficulty jump.

## What changes: depth, not breadth

The topic list is identical to Level 1 — the same ten areas. The 2026 Level 2 weights are:

| Topic | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Financial Statement Analysis | 10–15% |
| Equity Investments | 10–15% |
| Fixed Income | 10–15% |
| Portfolio Management | 10–15% |
| Ethical and Professional Standards | 10–15% |
| Quantitative Methods | 5–10% |
| Economics | 5–10% |
| Corporate Issuers | 5–10% |
| Derivatives | 5–10% |
| Alternative Investments | 5–10% |

Same names, different demands. At Level 1 you learn what a dividend discount model is; at Level 2 you're handed a company and asked which valuation model is appropriate and what it spits out. At Level 1 you learn that pension accounting exists; at Level 2 you adjust the financials. Ethics stays heavily weighted and gets harder in a subtle way — the fact patterns are longer and the "obviously wrong" answer is gone.

CFA Institute says successful candidates report studying **over 300 hours per level**, and that guidance doesn't drop at Level 2. Most people find it goes up, because you can't compress an item set the way you can compress a flashcard.

## The most useful number CFA Institute publishes

Buried in every results press release is a split almost nobody talks about. For the May 2026 Level 2 exam, **first-time candidates passed at 48%, while candidates taking it after at least one deferral passed at 25%**. November 2025 was nearly identical: 50% versus 27%.

Deferring roughly halves your odds. Not because deferred candidates are worse students, but because momentum is most of this. Study, take it, move on. If you're picking between registering now and "waiting until I have more time," that data is the argument against waiting.

One logistical note that reinforces it: Level 2 is offered three times a year (May, August, and November), not four like Level 1. A failed or deferred attempt costs you more calendar than it does at Level 1.

## How to study for Level 2 differently

**Practice full item sets, timed, from early on.** This is the change that matters most. Reading notes and drilling isolated questions builds Level 1 skills. Level 2 tests whether you can extract the four relevant facts from two pages of noise in under twelve minutes. That's a separate skill and it only comes from reps.

**Read the questions before the vignette.** Most candidates who pass end up doing some version of this — skim the four questions, then read the case knowing what you're hunting for. Try it in practice, not for the first time on exam day.

**Get fluent with your calculator.** At 90 seconds a question you can fumble a keystroke. At Level 2, with multi-step valuation work inside a time budget, you can't.

**Don't neglect Ethics.** It's 10–15% of the exam — as heavy as FSA or Equity — and it's the cheapest weight to earn, because the Standards don't change year to year. If you want a refresher on the traps, our [CFA Level 1 ethics tips](/blog/cfa-level-1-ethics-tips) still apply; the reasoning is the same, the vignettes are just longer.

**Take mocks that mirror the real thing.** Not a question bank you graze — a full, timed, sit-down exam that tells you where you're weak while there's still time to fix it.
