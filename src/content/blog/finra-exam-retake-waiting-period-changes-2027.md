# FINRA Retake Waiting Period Dropping to 15 Days in 2027

Starting January 19, 2027, FINRA will cut the wait to retake a failed qualification exam from 30 days to 15 days after your first and second failed attempts, and from 180 days to 60 days after your third and any later failure within a two-year period. It applies to all FINRA qualification exams, including the SIE and the Series 7. FINRA announced the change in Regulatory Notice 26-18 on October 14, 2026.

If you're planning a licensing exam this winter, the details below can change when you schedule it.

## The old rules versus the new rules

| Failed attempt | Current wait | Wait starting January 19, 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 30 calendar days | 15 calendar days |
| 2nd | 30 calendar days | 15 calendar days |
| 3rd and any later (within two years) | 180 calendar days | 60 calendar days |

The "two-year period" matters. Per FINRA, failed attempts more than two years old aren't counted when figuring out how many times you've failed. Candidates taking an exam for the first time aren't affected, since the waiting periods only come into play after a failed attempt.

## Which exams are covered

FINRA says the revised waiting periods apply to all FINRA qualification exams, and the notice names the SIE explicitly. The wait only applies to the exam you failed. If you fail the SIE, you aren't subject to a waiting period for the Series 7, and vice versa.

One important exclusion: exams FINRA administers on behalf of other organizations may follow different rules. FINRA's own FAQ tells candidates enrolled in MSRB, NASAA, or NFA exams to check those organizations' websites. NASAA runs the state-level exams, so if you're retaking something like the Series 63, 65 or 66, don't assume the 15-day rule applies. Confirm it before you plan around it.

## What if you fail before January 19?

FINRA can't shorten anyone's waiting period before the effective date, and says it can't honor requests to reduce it early. But the change does reach people who fail shortly before it takes effect:

- **You fail before January 19 and haven't scheduled a retake appointment.** Your enrollment window will reflect the new, shorter waiting period.
- **You've already submitted a retake enrollment request but have no appointment.** FINRA says it will adjust your open testing window on January 19, 2027.
- **You've already booked an appointment.** FINRA says it can't adjust the window without canceling the appointment, and will contact affected individuals to go over options.

FINRA's own example: someone who fails a first or second attempt on January 13, 2027 and submits an enrollment request the next day ends up with a window that opens on January 28, 2027 once it's adjusted.

## Should you rush a retake just because you can?

A shorter wait is an option, not a plan. Fifteen days is enough time to get a second opinion on what went wrong, not usually enough to fix it. A retake costs real money: the SIE exam fee is $100 and the Series 7 fee is $395 per attempt, per FINRA, and the Series 7 also requires a sponsoring firm to enroll you. Burning an attempt because the calendar allowed it is an expensive way to find out you weren't ready. Fail twice and a third failure still triggers a two-month wait, even under the new rules.

A better way to use the shorter window:

1. **Read your score report first.** It breaks your performance down by topic area. On the Series 7, one function carries most of the exam: Function 3 is 91 of the 125 questions, so a weak showing there explains most failures. We go through this in [our Series 7 retake guide](/blog/failed-series-7-retake-rules).
2. **Spend the 15 days on your weakest areas only.** Don't re-read the whole book. Do targeted questions and review every miss.
3. **Take a full-length timed practice exam before you re-book.** Book the retake when you're clearing the 72 passing mark on the Series 7 (70 on the SIE) comfortably, not barely. ## The bottom line

Exam failures will cost less time starting January 19, 2027, but not less money. The benefit goes to people who use the shorter gap for focused review rather than a quick rebook. If you pass on the first try, none of this matters, so the best strategy is still to be overprepared the first time.
